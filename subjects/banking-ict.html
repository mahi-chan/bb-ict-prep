    Banking ICT

    This subject is the most directly relevant to the job. Core banking systems, BACH/BEFTN/RTGS, card switching/EMV, mobile financial services, internet banking, BCP/DR and compliance form the bulk of the questions — all specific to Bangladesh's banking infrastructure.

    1. Core banking systems

    A Core Banking Solution (CBS) is the centralised software that processes daily banking transactions — deposits, withdrawals, loans, interest — in real time across all branches.

    FeatureDescription
    Centralised databaseAll branches share a single ledger — any branch, any transaction
    Real-time processingBalance updated immediately after transaction
    ModulesDeposits, loans, trade finance, treasury, GL
    IntegrationConnects to ATM switch, internet banking, BACH, BEFTN, RTGS
    Vendors in BDFlexcube (Oracle), Temenos T24, Finacle (Infosys), Ababil (local)
    CBS replaced the old branch-level ledger system. Before CBS, a customer could only transact at their "home branch". After CBS, any-branch banking is possible because the database is centralised.

    2. Payment systems (BACH, BEFTN, RTGS)

    Bangladesh Bank operates three electronic payment systems:

    SystemFull formSettlementUse case
    BACHBangladesh Automated Clearing HouseEnd-of-day net settlementCheque clearing (images, not physical)
    BEFTNBangladesh Electronic Funds Transfer NetworkSame-day batch settlementSalary disbursement, vendor payments, small transfers
    RTGSReal Time Gross SettlementReal-time, transaction-by-transactionHigh-value transfers (≥ BDT 1 lakh), interbank
    BACH = net settlement (offsets debits and credits, settles the difference)
    RTGS = gross settlement (each transaction settled individually, immediately)
    RTGS eliminates settlement risk but requires more liquidity

    NPS (National Payment Switch Bangladesh): interoperability platform connecting all banks, MFS providers, and payment systems. Enables interoperable transactions — e.g. bKash-to-bank, one bank's ATM to another's card.

    SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication): used for international transfers. Messages (MT103 for customer transfers, MT202 for bank-to-bank). The 2016 Bangladesh Bank heist exploited SWIFT message forgery.

    BACH processes cheques via images (truncation) — the physical cheque doesn't travel between banks. This sped up clearing from days to same-day or next-day.

    3. Cards and switching

    Card typeFundingFeature
    Debit cardLinked to bank account (spend what you have)ATM withdrawal, POS purchase
    Credit cardCredit line from issuer (spend now, pay later)Interest-free period, rewards
    Prepaid cardPre-loaded with fundsNo bank account needed; gift cards

    EMV chip cards replaced magnetic stripe to reduce fraud. The chip generates a unique transaction code each time → cannot be cloned like mag stripe. BB mandated EMV migration for all banks.

    Card transaction flow:

    1. Cardholder presents card at POS/ATM
    2. Acquirer (merchant's bank) routes request through card network (Visa/Mastercard/NPS)
    3. Issuer (cardholder's bank) authorises or declines
    4. Settlement: funds move from issuer to acquirer (via clearing network)

    ATM switch: connects all ATMs in the country. In Bangladesh, NPS acts as the national ATM switch, enabling interbank ATM transactions.

    The four-party model: cardholder → issuer → card network → acquirer → merchant. Interchange fee flows from acquirer to issuer. Understanding this flow is essential for banking ICT questions.

    4. Mobile financial services

    MFS enables financial transactions via mobile phone, targeting the unbanked population. Bangladesh is a global leader in MFS adoption.

    ProviderOperatorLaunchModel
    bKashBrac Bank subsidiary2011Bank-led MFS
    NagadBangladesh Post Office (digital)2019Government-backed
    RocketDutch-Bangla Bank2011Bank-led
    UpayUCB2019Bank-led

    MFS services: cash-in/cash-out (via agents), P2P transfer, merchant payment, salary disbursement, utility bill payment, government disbursements (social safety net).

    Technology: USSD (*247#) for feature phones, apps for smartphones. KYC via NID (National ID). BB regulates MFS through the Payment and Settlement Systems Division.

    Key statistics: 180M+ registered accounts, 1.5M+ agents, daily transaction volume exceeding BDT 3,000 crore.

    Cash-out charge (bKash) = 1.85% of withdrawal amount
    Example: BDT 10,000 cash-out → charge = 10,000 × 0.0185 = BDT 185
    Agent commission ≈ 1.0% = BDT 100 | Provider revenue ≈ 0.85% = BDT 85
    An agent handling 50 cash-outs/day earns ~BDT 5,000/day — this unit economics sustains the 1.5M-agent network
    MFS transformed financial inclusion in Bangladesh — over 50% of adults now have access to financial services via mobile. The agent network is the backbone — agents are essentially human ATMs in every village.

    5. Digital and internet banking

    Internet banking lets customers access banking services via web browser. Mobile banking via dedicated apps. Both require:

    • Multi-factor authentication (password + OTP)
    • SSL/TLS encryption for all data in transit
    • Transaction limits and velocity checks
    • Session timeout and secure logout

    Open Banking (emerging concept): banks expose APIs to authorised third parties, enabling fintech apps to access customer data (with consent) and initiate payments. Regulated by PSD2 in Europe; Bangladesh is developing its framework.

    QR code payments: Bangla QR (interoperable QR standard mandated by BB) enables merchant payments via any bank/MFS app scanning a single QR code.

    BB's guidelines require banks to implement real-time fraud monitoring for internet banking, including IP geolocation checks, device fingerprinting and unusual transaction pattern detection.

    6. Banking security and compliance

    Framework/StandardScope
    PCI-DSSPayment card data security — required for all card processors
    ISO 27001Information Security Management System (ISMS)
    BB ICT GuidelinesBangladesh Bank's ICT security guidelines for banks
    SWIFT CSPSWIFT Customer Security Programme — mandatory controls
    Basel IIIOperational risk framework (includes IT risk)

    BB ICT Guidelines mandate: CISO appointment, annual VAPT (vulnerability assessment and penetration testing), SOC (Security Operations Centre), cyber incident reporting within 24 hours, data classification, and access control policies.

    Anti-Money Laundering (AML) technology: transaction monitoring systems flag suspicious patterns (large cash deposits, rapid fund movements, structuring). KYC/eKYC for customer onboarding.

    The 2016 Bangladesh Bank SWIFT heist ($81M stolen from reserves at NY Fed) was a watershed moment. It led to mandatory SWIFT CSP compliance, enhanced network segmentation, and dedicated cybersecurity teams at all banks.

    7. Business continuity

    BCP (Business Continuity Plan) ensures critical banking operations continue during and after a disaster. DR (Disaster Recovery) focuses specifically on IT systems recovery.

    DR site typeReady stateRTOCost
    Hot siteFully operational, real-time data syncMinutes to hoursHighest
    Warm siteEquipment ready, periodic data backupHours to 1 dayMedium
    Cold siteSpace only, no equipment pre-installedDays to weeksLowest

    BB requirement: all scheduled banks must have a geographically separate DR site (minimum 30 km from primary), annual DR drills, and documented BCP reviewed yearly.

    RPO (Recovery Point Objective) = max tolerable data loss
    RTO (Recovery Time Objective) = max tolerable downtime
    For core banking: typical RPO = 0 (zero data loss), RTO = 4 hours
    BB mandates annual DR drills where banks actually fail over to the DR site and process transactions for a minimum period. This ensures the DR site works in practice, not just on paper.

    8. Emerging finance and fintech

    Fintech categories relevant to Bangladesh banking:

    • Payment tech: QR payments, contactless (NFC), wallets
    • Lending tech: digital lending, credit scoring via alternative data
    • Regtech: AML/KYC automation, regulatory reporting
    • Insurtech: digital insurance distribution
    • Wealthtech: robo-advisory, digital investment platforms

    BB Regulatory Sandbox: allows fintech companies to test innovative products in a controlled environment with regulatory oversight before full launch.

    CBDC (Central Bank Digital Currency): BB is studying the feasibility of a digital taka. Unlike cryptocurrency, CBDC is government-issued legal tender in digital form.

    The BB exam increasingly asks about fintech and its regulation. Key distinction: fintech supplements traditional banking (BB's position), it doesn't replace the regulated banking system. All fintech services touching payments must go through a licensed bank or MFS provider.

    S3What is MFS? Explain its role in financial inclusion in Bangladesh.standard repeat

    MFS (Mobile Financial Services) provides banking services via mobile phones through a network of human agents.

    Financial inclusion impact:

    • 180M+ registered accounts — more than bank accounts in the country
    • 1.5M+ agents reach every union/village — human ATMs where banks don't have branches
    • Government social safety net payments (old age allowance, widow allowance) disbursed via MFS
    • Garment worker salary disbursement — safer than cash
    • During COVID-19, MFS was critical for emergency cash transfers

    Regulatory framework: BB licenses MFS providers; must be bank-led (subsidiary or department); KYC via NID; transaction limits: daily BDT 25,000, monthly BDT 200,000.

    S4What is EMV? Why is it more secure than magnetic stripe cards?standard repeat

    EMV (Europay, Mastercard, Visa) is the global standard for chip-based payment cards.

    FeatureMag stripeEMV chip
    DataStatic — same data every swipeDynamic — unique cryptogram per transaction
    CloningEasy (skimmer copies data)Extremely difficult (cryptographic keys)
    AuthenticationSignature (easily forged)PIN (chip + PIN)
    Fraud liabilityIssuer bearsShifts to non-EMV party

    BB mandated full EMV migration for all banks. Contactless (NFC) builds on EMV with tap-to-pay convenience.

    S5Explain BCP and DR for a bank. What are RPO and RTO?standard repeat

    BCP covers all aspects of business continuity: people, processes, technology, facilities. DR is the IT component of BCP.

    RPO = max data loss tolerance (e.g. 0 for real-time replication)
    RTO = max downtime tolerance (e.g. 4 hours for core banking)

    BB requirements:

    • Geographically separate DR site (≥ 30 km from primary)
    • Annual DR drill with actual failover
    • Documented BCP reviewed yearly by board
    • Backup strategy: real-time replication for critical systems, daily for others
    S6What is NPS (National Payment Switch Bangladesh)? Why is it important?standard repeat

    NPS is Bangladesh's national interoperability platform connecting all banks, MFS providers and payment service providers.

    Functions:

    • Interbank ATM routing — use any bank's ATM with any bank's card
    • Interoperable MFS — transfer between bKash, Nagad, bank accounts
    • Interoperable QR payments (Bangla QR)
    • Domestic card switching — alternative to Visa/Mastercard for domestic transactions

    Benefit: reduces dependence on international payment networks for domestic transactions, lowers costs and supports financial inclusion.

    S7What security measures should internet banking implement?standard repeat
    • Authentication: MFA (password + OTP), biometric for mobile
    • Encryption: TLS 1.2+ for data in transit, AES-256 for data at rest
    • Session management: timeout after inactivity, secure cookies (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite)
    • Transaction security: per-transaction OTP for fund transfers, daily/per-transaction limits
    • Fraud detection: IP geolocation, device fingerprinting, velocity checks, behavioural analytics
    • Input validation: prevent SQL injection, XSS via parameterised queries and output encoding
    • Monitoring: real-time transaction monitoring, SIEM, 24/7 SOC
    • Compliance: annual VAPT, PCI-DSS if handling cards, BB ICT guidelines
    S8Explain the card transaction flow from swipe to settlement.standard repeat
    1. Authorisation

      Cardholder taps/inserts card at POS → POS sends transaction to acquirer (merchant's bank) → acquirer routes through card network (Visa/MC/NPS) → issuer (cardholder's bank) checks balance/credit, approves or declines → response flows back to POS.

    2. Clearing

      At end of day, acquirer sends batch of approved transactions to card network. Network calculates interchange fees and net amounts per bank.

    3. Settlement

      Card network instructs BB (via settlement bank) to transfer funds: issuer pays acquirer (minus interchange fee). Acquirer credits merchant account (minus merchant discount rate).

    S9What is SWIFT? Describe the 2016 Bangladesh Bank heist and lessons learned.standard repeat

    SWIFT is a secure messaging network used by 11,000+ financial institutions globally for international fund transfers.

    2016 Heist:

    • Attackers compromised BB's SWIFT terminal via malware (likely spear phishing entry)
    • Sent 35 fraudulent transfer requests totalling $951M to New York Fed
    • 5 requests ($101M) were processed; $20M recovered; $81M successfully stolen (routed to Philippines)
    • A typo ("fandation" instead of "foundation") flagged one transfer, and a bank holiday stopped others

    Lessons: network segmentation (SWIFT terminal on isolated network), mandatory SWIFT CSP compliance, multi-person authorisation for large transfers, real-time monitoring, and enhanced cyber hygiene.

    S10What is PCI-DSS? List its 12 requirements.standard repeat

    PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) applies to all entities that store, process or transmit cardholder data.

    12 requirements (grouped into 6 goals):

    1. Install and maintain a firewall
    2. Don't use vendor-supplied default passwords
    3. Protect stored cardholder data
    4. Encrypt transmission of cardholder data over public networks
    5. Use and regularly update anti-virus
    6. Develop and maintain secure systems and applications
    7. Restrict access on a need-to-know basis
    8. Assign unique ID to each person with access
    9. Restrict physical access to cardholder data
    10. Track and monitor all access to network and cardholder data
    11. Regularly test security systems and processes
    12. Maintain a policy that addresses information security

    Practice questions

    10 questions
    P1Explain net settlement vs gross settlement. Which is used by BACH and which by RTGS?practice

    Net settlement (BACH/BEFTN): accumulates all debits and credits between banks throughout the day, then settles the net difference. Bank A owes Bank B ₹100, Bank B owes Bank A ₹70 → only ₹30 moves. Less liquidity needed.

    Gross settlement (RTGS): each transaction settled individually and immediately. No netting — full amount moves each time. Zero settlement risk because there's no accumulation of obligations.

    Trade-off: net settlement is liquidity-efficient but carries risk (if one bank defaults before settlement, the net positions change). Gross settlement eliminates this risk but requires banks to maintain higher reserves.

    P2What is Bangla QR? How does interoperable QR payment work?practice

    Bangla QR is BB's standardised QR code for merchant payments. One QR code works with any bank or MFS app.

    How it works:

    1. Merchant displays Bangla QR at their counter
    2. Customer scans with their bank/MFS app, enters amount
    3. Request routes through NPS to merchant's bank
    4. Funds transfer from customer's account to merchant's account (real-time or near real-time)

    Benefit: merchants need only one QR code instead of separate codes for each bank/MFS. This lowers the barrier for small merchants to accept digital payments.

    P5What is Open Banking? What are its potential benefits and risks for Bangladesh?practice

    Open Banking requires banks to share customer data (with consent) with authorised third-party providers via secure APIs.

    Benefits: fosters competition and innovation, enables comparison services, allows fintech to build on bank infrastructure, improves customer experience.

    Risks: data privacy concerns, API security vulnerabilities, customer consent management, systemic risk if critical APIs fail, and digital divide (excludes non-digital customers).

    BD context: BNDA provides the architectural framework. BB is studying implementation with a phased approach, likely starting with read-only account information APIs before payment initiation.

    P7What is AML (Anti-Money Laundering) in the context of banking ICT? How does technology help?practice

    AML refers to laws, regulations and technology that prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income.

    Technology's role:

    • Transaction monitoring: automated systems flag unusual patterns (large cash deposits, rapid transfers, structuring below reporting thresholds)
    • KYC/eKYC: digital identity verification using NID, biometrics, video KYC
    • Sanctions screening: real-time checks against OFAC, UN, and local sanctions lists
    • Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR): automated generation and filing with BFIU (Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit)
    • Network analysis: identifying related accounts and shell company structures
    P8What is the role of BFIU (Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit)?practice

    BFIU is Bangladesh's national agency for combating money laundering and terrorist financing, operating under Bangladesh Bank.

    Functions:

    • Receives and analyses suspicious transaction reports (STRs) and cash transaction reports (CTRs) from banks and financial institutions
    • Shares intelligence with law enforcement agencies
    • Issues AML/CFT guidelines and circulars
    • Conducts compliance inspections of banks
    • Represents Bangladesh in international AML bodies (APG, Egmont Group)

    Reporting thresholds: CTR for cash transactions above BDT 10 lakh; STR for any suspicious activity regardless of amount.

    P10What is the BB regulatory sandbox? Why is it important for fintech innovation?practice

    A regulatory sandbox is a controlled environment where fintech companies can test innovative products with real customers under BB's supervision, with relaxed regulatory requirements for a limited period.

    Why it matters:

    • Encourages innovation without compromising consumer protection
    • BB learns about new technologies before writing permanent regulations
    • Reduces time-to-market for beneficial fintech products
    • Creates a level playing field — startups can test alongside established banks
    • Failed experiments can be contained without systemic impact

    Process: apply to BB → evaluation → sandbox entry (6–12 months) → monitoring → exit: full license or withdrawal.
